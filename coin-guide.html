BRITISH CLASSIC

The Sovereign

The Sovereign is one of the defining names in British gold coinage. The modern Sovereign was introduced in 1817 during the reign of George III, although the name reaches back to a much earlier English gold coin first issued under Henry VII. Its long association with the monarchy, British commerce and the famous St George and the Dragon design gives it a distinctive place in numismatic history.

Collectors can encounter Sovereigns across monarchs, mints, dates and finishes. Proof issues, historic branch-mint coins, unusual dates and exceptional grades can therefore tell very different stories even when the denomination is the same.

BRITISH ICON

Britannia

Britannia has personified Britain for centuries and became the central figure of the Royal Mint's modern Britannia gold coin series in 1987. The familiar helmeted figure, often carrying a trident and shield, connects modern precious-metal coinage with a much older visual language of British identity.

Proof Britannias and special interpretations are collected not only for their gold content but also for finish, design, year, denomination, condition and certification. Changing artistic treatments of Britannia have made the series especially useful for collectors interested in the evolution of modern British coin design.

ROYAL HERITAGE

Royal Tudor Beasts

The Royal Tudor Beasts series draws on the heraldic creatures associated with Henry VIII and the Tudor dynasty, particularly the stone beasts that line the Moat Bridge at Hampton Court Palace. Each creature carries dynastic and political meaning, turning a modern gold coin series into a journey through Tudor symbolism.

For collectors, the attraction lies in building a connected series while comparing designs, denominations, weights, proof finishes, mintages and certified grades. The Lion of England is among the most recognisable heraldic subjects in the collection.

GREAT DESIGNS

Modern Masterpieces

Some modern British proof coins revisit celebrated designs from the history of coinage. These issues allow collectors to encounter historic artistic ideas through contemporary minting techniques, often in larger formats and highly finished proof surfaces.

The Gothic Crown associated with Queen Victoria is one of the most admired designs of the Victorian period. Modern reinterpretations connect nineteenth-century numismatic art with present-day craftsmanship, creating a bridge between historic design and modern collecting.

BRITISH HISTORY

Commemorative Gold Coins

British commemorative gold coins record people, places, anniversaries and events. Their subjects can range from royal milestones to literature, architecture and national history. Unlike a continuous bullion series, each commemorative issue can have its own design language, denomination, mintage and collector audience.

Collectors should distinguish the coin's legal denomination and precious-metal content from its numismatic characteristics. Mintage, presentation, condition, certification, provenance and demand can all be relevant when comparing commemorative issues.
