COIN GRADING & CERTIFICATION

Condition.
Certification.
Protection.

Independent grading developed to give the collectable-coin market more consistent authentication and assessment of condition. Services such as NGC and PCGS examine coins, assign grades using recognised numerical scales and encapsulate certified examples in protective holders.

Tamper-evident encapsulation is important because it helps protect a certified coin from handling and environmental exposure while keeping the certification associated with the individual coin. High-grade proof coins such as PF69 and PF70 can represent exceptional states of preservation, but grade is only part of the numismatic story.

Mintage, surviving population, provenance, eye appeal, historical importance, design and collector demand can all influence interest in a British gold coin. Aurum therefore presents certification in context rather than treating a grade as a promise of future value.

BRITISH GOLD COINS · LEGAL TENDER

What does legal tender actually mean?

Legal tender is a specific legal concept concerning the settlement of debts. UK coins, including authorised non-circulating commemorative and precious-metal coins, can have legal-tender status even though they are not intended to be spent in shops. Their denomination, such as £25, £50, £100 or £200, is a face value authorised for the coin. It should not be confused with the coin's gold content, collector value or market price.

For collectors, the important distinction is that a British gold coin can simultaneously be a physical precious-metal asset, a collectable numismatic object and sterling legal-tender currency. That legal status can be relevant to UK tax treatment, but different taxes apply different tests.

BRITISH GOLD COINS & UK TAX

Legal tender, CGT and VAT are related, but they are not the same test.

Capital Gains Tax and sterling legal tender

HMRC treats Sovereigns minted in 1837 and later and Britannia gold coins as sterling currency and therefore exempt from Capital Gains Tax. More generally, whether a particular coin benefits from this treatment depends on its status as sterling currency/legal tender under the applicable rules. This is distinct from simply being made of gold.

VAT and investment gold coins

VAT exemption follows the investment-gold rules, not legal-tender status alone. HMRC's criteria include, among other things, a coin minted after 1800, purity of at least 900 thousandths, current or former legal-tender status in its country of origin and a normal selling price within the prescribed relationship to the value of its gold content. Coins on HMRC's recognised investment-gold list can also qualify.

Face value is not market value

A £100 or £200 denomination on a British gold coin does not mean that is what the coin is worth to a collector. Its market value may reflect gold content together with condition, grade, rarity, mintage, provenance, design and collector demand.

Historic and ancient coins

Coins that are not legal tender require separate CGT consideration and may instead fall within the rules for chattels. Historic or ancient status by itself does not create a blanket tax exemption.

Tax treatment can change and depends on the exact coin and individual circumstances. This is general information, not personal tax or investment advice. No IHT exemption is claimed.
